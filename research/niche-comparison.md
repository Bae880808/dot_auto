# Tool niche comparison

Research date: 2026-10-17. Sources: live web searches and direct page text inspection. No interactive browser tests were performed in this subtask. No keyword volumes, difficulty scores, RPMs, conversion rates, local SERP rankings, or revenue estimates were obtained. Search results show existing supply and the wording of discoverable problems, not quantified demand. Country reach and ad eligibility remain unverified.

## Recommendation

Choose **video delivery and storage planning for independent editors and small production teams**, with English and Simplified Chinese interfaces. The choice is conditional on implementation simplicity, and a coherent recurring workflow, NOT a claim that this is the least competitive or easiest money.

Positioning: “Know whether your export fits, how long delivery takes, and how much project storage you need.” A lightweight browser workbench connects the same duration, bitrate, audio, and capacity values across tools. Do not market as a video encoder, uploader, file validator, or guaranteed platform-compliance checker.

Prioritize a useful free MVP. Advertising is a potential later revenue source only after real traffic and network eligibility exist. Subscription for simple arithmetic is weakly supported. Optional project presets, saved job history, branded planning reports, and team reuse are monetization hypotheses, not promised demand. A paid one-time report/workflow product may fit better than forced monthly billing. No paid APIs are necessary for core math.

## Three finalists

### 1. Video delivery and storage planning — recommended

- Observable need: camera manufacturers publish their own recording/storage calculators; independent apps bundle these tasks. This is evidence of a genuine workflow, not search-volume validation.
- Free saturation: high. ARRI offers camera-specific data-rate/duration calculations. AJA DataCalc offers storage/time modes. CineMath covers bitrate, upload time, storage, aspect ratio and timecode tasks. Chinese search also surfaced existing file-size and bitrate calculators, so translation alone is not a moat.
- Repeat-use hypothesis: an editor changes duration, delivery limit, bitrate, copies, and network speed for each job; saved project settings can reduce repeat entry. Must validate retention rather than assume it.
- Paid evidence: DIT Media Calculator advertises USD 49.99 one-time Full Unlock and USD 9.99/year optional camera-library updates. These are offered prices, not verified purchases, customers, or revenue. The paid object is a fuller planning workflow and reports, not just a conversion formula.
- Localization: equations are largely country-neutral. English serves US/UK/AU; Simplified Chinese serves Chinese-language users. Use MB versus MiB, Mbps versus MB/s, 23.976/24/25/29.97/30/50/59.94/60 fps explicitly. Let users select frame rate rather than equating country with a mandatory standard. Exclude exchange-rate APIs.
- Risks: bitrate varies with codec/content; connection speed is not sustained throughput; audio and container overhead matter; full-size media uploads create unnecessary cost and privacy risk; platform limits change by account/product/version. Do not copy unverified Douyin/Bilibili upload caps from generic calculators. A user-entered file limit is safer.
- Best initial differentiation hypothesis: browser-based, bilingual, transparent units and assumptions, connected export-to-delivery plan, useful low-bandwidth mobile layout, and shareable/printable parameter summary. Competitor testing is still required to show an actual gap.

### 2. FDM filament and repeat-batch planning — runner-up

- Observable need: Prusa maintains a price calculator and official spool-measurement guidance. Multiple direct businesses sell saved inventory and planning workflows.
- Free saturation: very high, including Chinese incumbents. Prusa already provides editable cost components, G-code input and printable/shareable summaries. PrintReady3D, Tripo, LayerCalc and MakerCalc compete with much of the proposed feature set. Generic print-cost SEO is not a differentiated wedge.
- Repeat use: potentially strong for repeat sellers planning stock, waste and batches; weaker for occasional hobbyists. Cannot assume integration-free manual input will remain attractive.
- Paid evidence: Spoolstock offers USD 1.99/month or USD 19.99/year after its free allowance; SimplyPrint offers a standalone filament manager at USD 5.99/month; LayerMath offers GBP 9/month around a free calculator. These support pricing hypotheses for retained inventory/workspace, not willingness to pay for a fresh clone.
- Localization: g/kg/mm/metres and user-entered local currency/electricity make US/UK/AU/China fairly easy. Currency symbols are labels, not automatic conversion. Avoid maintaining marketplace fees/tax rules in MVP.
- Risks: wrong spool tare/density, double-counted purge, print failures that do not consume a full attempt, and optimistic capacity estimates. Input slicer-measured values rather than promise geometry-derived printing time. No printer control or credentials in MVP.
- Eight-tool possible scope: job cost; grams-to-length; spool remaining; complete parts per spool; multi-material purge budget; reprint allowance; batch machine-hour planner; spool unit-price comparison.

### 3. Soft-furnishing fabric and cut planning — reject for first MVP

- Observable need: Sailrite and Spotlight supply fabric calculators and instructions alongside products.
- Free saturation: very high and unusually close to the proposed bilingual proposition. Workroom Tools explicitly offers English/Simplified Chinese, local save, project exports, CSV cut lists and printing across many furnishing tools. Sailrite has sophisticated visual cushion layouts and pattern placement.
- Repeat use: plausible for professional workrooms, occasional for household DIY. Paid quoting/workroom software exists, but sufficient directly inspected pricing was not established here.
- Localization: US inch/yard vs UK/AU/China metric, fabric width, fullness, hem construction and continuous-drop versus width-bought fabric change logic, not just labels. Chinese fixed-height/fixed-width procurement models need real domain expertise.
- Risks: material cutting mistakes are costly; pattern repeat, nap, shrinkage and seam placement defeat naive area division. Interactive cutting layouts are a substantially bigger quality burden than scalar media calculations.
- Conclusion: not a defensible fast-launch gap without real workroom expertise or an explicitly different workflow.

## Eight canonical media tools (final selection)

1. Video file size: duration + video and audio bitrate → estimated file size with explicit overhead.
2. Target-size bitrate budget: target bytes and duration → video bitrate after audio and overhead allowances.
3. Upload/transfer time: bytes and sustained throughput → duration with explicit efficiency assumption.
4. Delivery-deadline budget: available time minus user-entered export/processing reserve → latest upload start, required upload speed, or maximum deliverable bytes.
5. Recording capacity: usable card space and recording bitrate → recording duration or capacity required.
6. Multicamera shoot storage: independent camera rows, recorded hours, separate audio, and selected copy counts → per-day/project storage.
7. Offload and verified-copy plan: data size, destinations, measured copy and verification-read rates → sequential planning time. Does NOT copy or verify any files.
8. Proxy/storage tradeoff: original and proxy bitrates/durations/copy counts → additional storage and reduced transfer scenario, while originals are retained.

These final eight supersede the earlier exploratory audio/aspect/render possibilities. Unit conversion is embedded, not padded into separate tools. Offload and proxy tools require clear differences from basic transfer/file-size pages: multi-step verification planning and retained-original workflow comparison respectively.

All eight can compute locally without accounts, paid APIs, file upload or external services. A common project bar carries shared inputs, but each tool remains independently usable and indexable. Do not split equivalent formulas into dozens of thin pages. See media-formula-spec.md for assumptions and fixtures; media-keywords-100.csv contains the final 100 mapped candidate terms.

## Commercial validation boundaries

- Do not equate supply, app pricing, or a manufacturer's calculator with measured search demand.
- Do not say the niche is low competition; these searches found substantial competition.
- Do not use Frame.io storage/collaboration subscriptions as proof that customers pay for calculators.
- English and Chinese support does not establish local distribution, ad delivery, publisher eligibility, payments or mainland-China access. Treat China monetization as a separate validation task. No blanket assertion about AdSense coverage is made here.
- First validate search impressions, completed calculations, repeat visits and saved-plan use. Only then evaluate ads or paid workflow. Prices, RPM and earnings forecasts need actual observations or clearly labeled scenario inputs.

## DIT ownership and purchase terms verification

Direct official landing page inspected 2026-10-17: identifies the developer as RedPixl s.r.o.; describes a USD 49.99 one-time Full Unlock covering Mac, iPad and iPhone; says the unlock does not expire. USD 9.99/year is a separate optional camera-library-update subscription; cancelling retains what is already owned. The free version is restricted to ARRI ALEXA Mini LF but includes the storage/card/backup/LTO math. This is not a USD 49.99 subscription. These are the vendor's stated terms, not an independently tested purchase. The original App Store link retrieval failed, but a subsequent direct read of https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dit-media-calculator/id6791876002 succeeded: the US listing independently confirms Seller RedPixl s.r.o., Full Unlock $49.99 and Camera Library Updates $9.99. Billing cadence is explicit on the vendor page, not in the App Store price list. The App Store says there are not enough ratings/reviews for an overview. Checkout and non-US territory prices were not tested. Do not claim sales numbers, that the app was purchased, or that its terms were accepted.
